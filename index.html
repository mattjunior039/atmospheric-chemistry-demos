Fly up through the atmosphere

0 km
Temperature Pressure Air below you

Predict: Temperature falls as we climb. Why does it start rising again above about 20 km?
Ozone absorbs UV light (O₃ + hν → O₂ + O) and turns that energy into heat, so the stratosphere warms with height. The thermosphere is also hot because O₂, N₂ and O absorb very high-energy UV.
Predict: At what altitude is half of the atmosphere below you?
At only about 5.5 km. Pressure falls roughly exponentially, so about 99% of the air is below about 31 km. Use the slider to check.

Predict: Does it take more energy to break O₂ into two O atoms or to remove an electron from O₂? Which needs the shorter wavelength?
Photoionization (1205 kJ/mol, λ ≤ 99 nm) needs much more energy than photodissociation (495 kJ/mol, λ ≤ 242 nm). Only the shortest-wavelength UV can ionize molecules, and that light is absorbed high in the thermosphere.

A photon must have at least the threshold energy, and the molecule must also be able to absorb that wavelength. Energy values come from Table 18.3 and the textbook bond energies.

Predict: How many ozone molecules can one Cl atom destroy before it is trapped in a reservoir such as HCl?
In this scaled-down model, the answer is tens. In the real stratosphere, it is on the order of 100,000. Because CFCs last for decades, the 1987 Montreal Protocol ban takes decades to repair the ozone layer. The Antarctic ozone hole is projected to recover around the 2060s.

A smoggy day in the city

NO NO₂ O₃

Predict: Traffic peaks around 7–9 a.m. Why does ozone peak in the afternoon?
O₃ is a secondary pollutant. Cars emit NO, which becomes brown NO₂. Sunlight then splits NO₂ (λ < 393 nm) into NO + O, and O + O₂ + M → O₃ + M. That takes time and strong sunlight, so ozone peaks around midday and in the afternoon.
Predict: Do catalytic converters filter pollutants out?
No. They chemically convert pollutants: CO and hydrocarbons become CO₂ + H₂O, and NO becomes N₂, using Pt, Pd and Rh catalysts. Turn them on and watch the curves drop.

This is a qualitative model of a typical sunny urban day, not a real measurement.

Why do CO₂ and H₂O trap heat, but N₂ and O₂ do not?

The curve shows Earth's infrared glow, modeled as a 288 K blackbody. The line marks where this vibration would absorb. Motion is exaggerated, and the arrow points toward the δ⁻ end.

Predict: N₂ and O₂ make up 99% of the air. Why are they not greenhouse gases?
A molecule absorbs IR only if its vibration changes its dipole moment. N₂ and O₂ are symmetric, so stretching never creates a dipole. CO₂ has no permanent dipole, but its bend and asymmetric stretch do change the dipole. The 15 µm bend sits near the peak of Earth's IR glow.

Discuss: The model learned the trend and the seasonal cycle. Why does CO₂ fall each northern summer? What could make the forecast wrong?
In northern summer, plants take in CO₂; in winter, decay and fuel use release it. The model only fits patterns using least-squares learning. It does not understand physics, emissions policy, volcanoes or El Niño. Try training only up to 1990 and see how far a straight-line model misses the present.
