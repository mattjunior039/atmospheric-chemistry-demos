Ozone vs. chlorine: one catalyst, many victims

Ozone O₃ destroyed by Cl Active Cl / ClO Cl atoms released O₃ destroyed per Cl
CF₂Cl₂ + hν → CF₂Cl + Cl
Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂
ClO + O → Cl + O₂
Net: O₃ + O → 2 O₂  (Cl is regenerated, so it is a catalyst)
Predict: How many ozone molecules can one Cl atom destroy before it is trapped in a reservoir such as HCl?
In this scaled-down model, the answer is tens. In the real stratosphere, it is on the order of 100,000. Because CFCs last for decades, the 1987 Montreal Protocol ban takes decades to repair the ozone layer. The Antarctic ozone hole is projected to recover around the 2060s.

Why do CO₂ and H₂O trap heat, but N₂ and O₂ do not?

The curve shows Earth's infrared glow, modeled as a 288 K blackbody. The line marks where this vibration would absorb. Motion is exaggerated, and the arrow points toward the δ⁻ end.

Predict: N₂ and O₂ make up 99% of the air. Why are they not greenhouse gases?
A molecule absorbs IR only if its vibration changes its dipole moment. N₂ and O₂ are symmetric, so stretching never creates a dipole. CO₂ has no permanent dipole, but its bend and asymmetric stretch do change the dipole. The 15 µm bend sits near the peak of Earth's IR glow.
